NEW-CANDIDATE SEARCH, HIGH SEVERITY FIRST
- Farm to post-harvest: select exact crop/region and reason for spoilage, using NABCONS, FAO, state agricultural or cold-chain data. Examine whether lack of storage/roads dominates any information gap.
- Dark stores and supermarket perishables: replenishment, shelf life, markdown and cancellations; look for permissioned data, not aggregate q-commerce GMV.
- Hospital/elder-care food service / nutrition: separately examine special diets and plate waste under safety guardrails.
- Waste after disposal: compost, biogas/animal feed where lawful, handling contamination; don't divert edible food merely to claim circularity.
- Supply-chain traceability, cold-chain events, predictive scheduling, production planning: already served by established ERP/IoT/logistics solutions; investigate specific failures, competitors and feasible first party data.
- AI infrastructure and environmental footprint: for eventual hackathon compare net avoided waste with cloud/AI usage burden; don't claim savings from predictions alone.

7. Cost/latency/evaluation (accuracy, false food-safety assurances, tool failures, user effort, environmental footprint) and hackathon tech novelty vs ordinary Gemini conversation.

RUN 01 | 14 October 2026, 09:45 IST | FOOD-06-TOMATO-01 | NARROW
Candidate: tomato losses from harvest and grading to first market, Anand/Kheda (Gujarat). Users: tomato growers, FPOs, traders, transporters. Hypothesis, not established: time-sensitive buyer/transport/quality information might fail to meet at the decision point. Distinguish this from bruising, lack of cold storage, price collapse and transport costs, which software alone cannot fix.


NEW PRIMARY EVIDENCE: A peer-reviewed original study published 2 July 2026 surveyed 270 farmers (90 each tomato, onion, potato) in six purposively selected Operation Greens districts during 2022–23, plus 15 middlemen per crop in Table 2. For Gujarat tomato clusters (Anand/Kheda), reported entire-chain quantity loss was 21.51 +/- 1.004%, producer-stage loss 12.36 +/- 0.674%, middlemen-stage loss 8.15 +/- 0.230%; value loss 13.85 +/- 0.796%. The stages have different denominators and do not sum straightforwardly. Observational study, NOT national prevalence or experimental AI savings. Article has an apparent editorial inconsistency calling Gujarat potato despite tables indicating tomato; treat detailed regression signs cautiously. Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sustainable-food-systems/articles/10.3389/fsufs.2026.1773566/full (2026-07-02).


INDEPENDENT GOVERNMENT BENCHMARK: NABCONS 2020–22 national tomato study, reported by Ministry of Food Processing Industries/PIB in 2025, gives 8.37% farm-operation loss and 3.25% market-level loss. These figures use different geography and methods than the Gujarat cluster study; NOT a time trend and not directly comparable. Overall vegetable loss was 11.97 million metric tonnes across vegetables, not tomatoes alone. Source: https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2151371&lang=2&reg=48 . A 2023 tomato supply-chain paper also points to physical handling and limited cold-chain capacity, undermining any software-only causal assumption: https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/2/1331 .


ENVIRONMENT/SOCIAL: losses after water, land, fertilizer, fuel and farm labor have been spent; plausible farmer-income and resource-efficiency effects, but no crop-specific net emissions, water or nutrition benefits from an AI intervention measured. Count weighed edible kg lost per harvested kg, realized farmer margins, and new transport/cold-chain/compute burdens. Do not multiply national production by cluster loss or call it an addressable market.


COMPETITIVE COUNTEREVIDENCE (verified 14 Oct 2026): Government e-NAM already supports APMC market linkage, quality-based auctions and price discovery (https://www.enam.gov.in/web/). Ninjacart already offers farmer/trader/retailer logistics, supply-chain information and inventory tools (https://ninjacart.com/). Ninjacart's historical Harvest the Farms program explicitly described purchasing surplus produce for discounted resale (https://ninjacart.com/blog/harvest-the-farms-htf-initiative-by-ninjacart/). Claims on supplier site are product claims, not independently validated reductions. Cheap physical alternatives: better crates, shade, cooling, processor contracts, farmer-producer pooling, and phone coordination. A generic price-alert or surplus marketplace is not novel.


GEMINI TECH GATE (Google docs checked 14 Oct 2026): Function calling generates a structured request; application code authenticates and executes external actions. It cannot independently commit a farmer to a sale (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/function-calling). Structured outputs can standardize multilingual lot notes, invoice data and buyer quotes, but JSON validity does not prove factual correctness (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/structured-output). ADK sequential workflows and shared session state exist, but their orchestration is deterministic and not automatically innovative (https://adk.dev/agents/workflow-agents/sequential-agents/ ; https://adk.dev/sessions/state/). Search grounding retrieves public information, not private buyer or transport commitments (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/google-search). Token/tool fees require instrumentation; no compute or carbon estimate has been measured (https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing). Potential AI value: reconcile unstructured, time-sensitive lot, quality, logistics and buyer records with explicit human authorization. Rules, sensors, forecasting and optimization may be better for other steps. Model comparison NOT RUN.


DATA ACCESS / KILL GATES: No permissioned FPO transaction data, actual buyer/transport offers, recorded rejected lots, partner API or field experiment is available yet. Public government statistics do not establish a real-time workflow gap. Physical storage and handling dominate many documented losses; e-NAM and Ninjacart already serve market information and logistics. No proof that information latency causes a measurable subset of spoilage. The 2026 article mentions underlying data in repositories or supplements, but directly usable raw lot-level records are not verified.


NEXT TEST (NOT RUN): obtain 20–30 historical consented tomato lots from one FPO or aggregator, with harvested and rejected kg, grade, date, buyer offers, freight, storage, sale outcome and rejection reasons. Classify physical damage vs price/economics vs avoidable decision delay. For genuinely information-addressable cases compare (A) existing e-NAM and phone coordination, (B) rules/optimization, (C) Gemini parsing and tool orchestration with identical inputs. Pass only if a real feasible alternative existed at the time and Gemini improves correctness or user effort beyond both baselines. Include new transport, energy and inference costs in net environmental effect. Otherwise reject the GenAI case.


RESEARCH VERDICT: NARROW; environmental severity high but addressable residual gap unknown. Problem Value Score /100 withheld. Hackathon judging 40/25/25/10 separate and not scored. Next research stage: source data/access and root-cause validation, not product design. Preserve original broad FOOD-06 deferral until specific new evidence warrants reopening.


RUN 02 | 14 Oct 2026, 10:40 IST | FOOD-06-ONION-02 | NARROW
New challenger: Maharashtra rabi onion storage, curing and release decisions. Distinct from Run 01 tomato harvest-to-market. Affected users: onion growers, cooperatives, warehouse managers, buyers, consumers. Decisions: curing, grading, storage method, monitoring, sale timing. Underlying stakes: edible onion spoilage, farmer revenue, price volatility and embedded agricultural resources. Actual information-addressable share UNKNOWN.
ORIGINAL WORKFLOW STUDY: Sahu et al., Indian Journal of Agricultural Sciences (published Jan 2022; field data 2020), n=60 growers in four selected villages in Pune and Nashik. Shade curing was known by 40/60 but practiced by 17/60 (28.22%). Suitable ventilated storage known by 46/60 but practiced by 14/60 (23.24%). Market intelligence known by 33/60 but used by 10/60 (16.6%). Labor, cost, access and infrastructure also limited adoption; information alone is not the proven cause. Original PDF: https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJAgS/article/download/120802/46283/336712
GOVERNMENT BENCHMARK: NABCONS 2020-22 national onion estimates reported by PIB Aug 2025: 5.31% farm-operation loss, 1.96% market-stage loss; different denominators and stages, not directly additive or comparable to seasonal storage studies. https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2151572&lang=2&reg=48
ICAR-DOGR demonstration after 120 days: 7.14% loss in controlled low-energy storage, 5.00% conventional cold storage, 54.89% ventilated storage in the compared conditions. This is not a representative national rate or AI outcome; original experimental sample and independent replication unverified. Physical infrastructure is a powerful competing explanation. https://icar.org.in/en/node/4701

RUN 03 | 14 October 2026, 11:47 IST | FOOD-06-BANANA-03 | NARROW — new crop and stage, distinct from tomato/onion
HYPOTHESIS / STAKEHOLDER: Northeast India banana farmers and aggregators (East Garo Hills, Meghalaya; Goalpara, Assam), especially first-mile post-harvest handling, storage and market dispatch. Task: after a bunch is harvested, decide safe packing, storage, collection and actual sale/transport before deterioration. Possible failure: smallholders with limited storage and credit cannot execute timely, feasible alternatives when a buyer or truck changes. This is NOT proof that AI coordination is missing; physical assets and buyer access may dominate.


INDEPENDENT PRIMARY EVIDENCE (NOT THE SAME DENOMINATOR): Das (World Food Policy, published 13 May 2025, doi:10.1002/wfp2.70006) surveyed 480 banana-farming households using multistage sampling in East Garo Hills and Goalpara, analyzing pre-, during- and post-COVID phases with ordered probit and robustness checks. Reported post-harvest losses 23.74% during COVID disruption and 8.55% post-pandemic. The phases were assessed in a household survey, not verified by an independent prospective weighing experiment. Identified factors include household income, landholdings, access to credit, transport disruption, environmental conditions and inadequate storage. Study's supporting material is advertised as a 224.2 KB CSV; author says data shared on reasonable request. The raw CSV's content, reuse license and lot-level transport timing have NOT been independently inspected. Same author published a 2023 study in the same region (n=100); treat it as potentially overlapping work, NOT independent replication. Source https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/wfp2.70006 ; earlier related study https://www.agriculturejournal.org/volume11number2/evaluation-of-postharvest-losses-of-banana-growers-in-north-east-india-an-ordered-probit-analysis/ .


SEPARATE GOVERNMENT BENCHMARK: MoFPI Rajya Sabha reply dated 1 Aug 2025, reporting NABCONS 2020–22 assessment of 54 crops, gives banana 5.17% farm-operations loss and 2.40% market-stage loss (stage-specific measures, NOT directly summed into a verified total). Government's 2024–25 second advance production estimate: 38.035 million tonnes bananas nationally. Do NOT multiply this later production estimate by local 8.55% or mix denominators/years to assert national tonnes of loss. Source https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=2151371&lang=2&reg=48 (Annexure I, II).


INDEPENDENT GEOGRAPHIC COUNTERCHECK: Velavan (2024, Asian Journal of Agricultural Extension, Economics & Sociology 42(6), 82–89) studied banana marketing channels in Tiruchirappalli, Erode and Coimbatore, Tamil Nadu. Reported selected-cultivar losses of 8.5–14.5 kg/tonne (0.85–1.45%, not 8.5–14.5%); one-third of intermediaries lacked storage and no packing method was followed in studied marketing channels. These are different stage/cultivar/site observations and cannot be averaged or treated as a time trend. Source https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/367991 ; https://doi.org/10.9734/ajaees/2024/v42i62465 .


2026 AGRONOMIC CONTEXT: ICAR researchers (Indian Horticulture, 19 March 2026) identify fragmented smallholder chains, inadequate cold-chain, uneven ripening, grading and access to aggregation, finance and market information for mango/banana, while proposing low-cost precooling, packaging, modular cold chains and cooperative systems. This is expert synthesis, not proof of unmet software need. Source https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndHort/article/view/177213 .


COMPETITION / WHAT ALREADY EXISTS: Superplum advertises temperature-controlled harvesting, monitored refrigerated transport, automated ripening and QR provenance including bananas; vendor capability claims, not independent measured impact (https://superplum.com/). BASE/EMPA with Innoterra announced banana-specific machine-learning defect detection for farmers in India in May 2022 (https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/industry/cons-products/food/base-empa-select-innoterra-to-for-early-detection-of-fruit-defects-to-banana-farmers-in-india/articleshow/91632755.cms); deployment extent and independent outcomes UNKNOWN. ICAR-National Research Centre for Banana already describes IoT cold-chain alerts, analytics and satellite/weather advisory as known technology (https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IndHort/article/view/163669). Existing physical/low-tech alternatives: crates, packaging, cooperative aggregation, accessible storage, reliable freight, phone/WhatsApp dispatch, contracts and credit. Therefore “AI identifies banana defects / monitors temperature / suggests buyers” is NOT a defensible novel proposition.


SPECIFIC RESIDUAL GAP TO TEST: Do viable, economically and physically feasible collection/buyer/packing choices exist at the moment of a loss-causing disruption but remain unexecuted because the smallholder and trader cannot reconcile dispersed commitments and context? Must establish such missed actions, not infer from loss percentage. Counter-hypothesis: capital, handling damage, roads, credit, price and cold rooms dominate, so better orchestration saves little edible food.

Model function calls are proposals executed by the application, NOT automatically authorized sales; see https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/function-calling and https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/structured-output . RUN 04 | 14 October 2026, 12:40 IST | FOOD-06-MARINE-FISH-04 | NARROW — NEW STAGE: KERALA DOMESTIC MARINE-FISH WHOLESALE/RETAIL COLD-CHAIN HANDOFF
DECISION: Keep as independent high-severity challenger, but NOT an advance to product build. Unlike Runs 01–03 (tomato/onion/banana), this run tests seafood arriving from landing sites into the first wholesale/retail market, where ice, handling, market capacity and demand interact. Do not conflate spoilage with price markdowns, discarded juveniles/bycatch, or legally usable processing byproducts.


PRECISE USER JOURNEY / ROOT-CAUSE HYPOTHESIS
Users: small-boat fishers and boat owners, landing-site auctioneers, wholesale commission agents, ice vendors, transporters, market sellers, fish buyers and fish consumers. At catch landing the auctioneer must match species/size/quality and quantity to buyers; the transporter needs available ice and cold transport; the wholesaler needs delivery and auction slots; the retailer must sell or refrigerate before unsafe deterioration. Hypothesis to falsify: a subset of otherwise usable, saleable fish is lost because truthful information about fish lots, available buyers, ice capacity and collection/auction commitments arrives too late. Competing hypothesis: ice scarcity, power/cold-chain capacity, handling, price monopsony, cash, labor and physical market bottlenecks are dominant; a software coordinator would merely observe unavoidable deterioration.


INDEPENDENT SEVERITY EVIDENCE, PRECISE DENOMINATORS
A. Government national estimate: NABCONS 2022 study (reference years 2020–22), as reported by Ministry of Food Processing Industries in Lok Sabha reply dated 4 Dec 2025, estimates 8.76% harvest and post-harvest loss for MARINE fisheries versus 4.86% inland fisheries. This is national, multi-stage and multi-species, NOT the Kerala market-only loss rate and NOT a recoverable AI share. Same source compares older ICAR-CIPHET 2015 marine estimate 10.52%, but methodology/time differences prevent treating the gap as a clean causal improvement. PIB: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2198688&lang=1&reg=3 .
B. Original market field study: Revathy K. Nair and A.V. Shibu, Journal of the Marine Biological Association of India 65(1), published 28 April 2023, studied wholesale, public and retail domestic fish markets in central Kerala using field assessment methods. Abstract says PHYSICAL loss accounted for 32% of the fish-loss COMPONENTS observed in selected markets. CRITICAL: 32% is NOT a 32% loss of all fish arriving. Full-text PDF indexed at https://mbai.org.in/uploads/manuscripts/65-1%20ART%20051759837521.pdf ; article https://www.mbai.org.in/jmbai-details/MjY1OQ%3D%3D/MTMw ; DOI 10.6024/jmbai.2023.65.1.2349-05 . Study cites inadequate icing, unhygienic handling, temperature variation and cold-chain gaps. Exact market counts, sampled tonnes, event-level observations and raw data/license were NOT verified because the publisher PDF fetch timed out; do not manufacture a denominator or call study independent weighing if the full methods are unavailable.
C. Independent ICAR price research: Salim et al., Indian Journal of Fisheries, published 31 March 2023, documents large Kerala fish-market price volatility across landing/wholesale/retail and cites supply-side constraints and monopsony conditions; recommends fish-market price information. It does NOT measure spoilage saved by better prices. https://epubs.icar.org.in/index.php/IJF/article/view/105674 (DOI 10.21077/ijf.2023.70.1.105674-18). Historical data not established as an available machine-readable dataset; article is CC BY-ND 4.0.
D. Independent scale context only: ICAR-CMFRI report published 2025 says India landed ~3.47m tonnes of marine fish in 2024, a 2% decline from 2023. It is NOT methodologically valid to multiply this later landing total by the 2020–22 loss percentage and call it actual 2024 loss or product TAM. https://eprints.cmfri.org.in/19094/
E. 2026 cross-country signal with lower evidence confidence: February 12, 2026 coverage describes a FAO/BOBP-IGO workshop study across 11 Asian countries, reportedly finding substantial losses from missing ice and cold-chain infrastructure, including 43.5% of reported losses occurring at landing sites. ORIGINAL study report, denominator and definitions NOT LOCATED, so no quantitative 43.5% claim is used as established fact; treat as a lead to retrieve original data. https://theprint.in/india/up-to-35-million-tonnes-of-fish-wasted-each-year-study/2853031/
