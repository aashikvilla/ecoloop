Mission: identify actual failures between DONOR > REGISTERED RECOVERY PARTNER > VERIFIED RECIPIENT > CONSUMED SAFE SERVINGS. This is NOT a generic donation marketplace. Read Research Control Room before each run.


HYPOTHESIS FOOD-05
Eligible unused safe food is offered, but a subset fails to reach intended users while still usable because time, volume, location, communication, capacity, dietary fit, documentation or pickup constraints conflict. Underlying gap is not proven after FSSAI IFSA and operational NGOs.


SOCIAL/HUMAN DIMENSION
Receiving kitchens/organizations may serve people with food insecurity, elders, children, shelter residents or patients. Safe and nutritionally appropriate acceptance is crucial. Do not treat orphanages/nursing homes/hospitals as unconsented destinations of random food. Donated kg != consumed meals != hunger reduction. Verify avoided disposal separately from substituting future food production.


LAW / SAFETY
FSSAI Food Safety and Standards (Recovery and Distribution of Surplus Food) Regulations 2019; food already served to consumers generally not eligible as safe surplus under the cited definition. Check current amendments/handling policies and legally appropriate facilities. Images cannot certify microbiological safety. Food temperature, allergens, packaging, time, custody and reporting all need authorized controls.


COMPETITION
FSSAI Indian Food Sharing Alliance, Robin Hood Army, Feeding India, local food banks and social welfare groups; Too Good To Go in served geographies, Goodr in US. Existing operations may already make simple matching non-novel. Transport and cold chain may dominate. Compare manual phone/WhatsApp/volunteer network.


RESEARCH QUEUE
1. Choose one metro and list verified local recovery agencies, requirements, schedules and categories served.
2. Find actual offer acceptance/rejection and pickup completion/failed transfer rates; separate expired/unsafe rejected food.
3. Investigate old-age homes, shelters, institutions as recipients with explicit approval and nutrition suitability; NGO throughput denominator.
4. Explore authorized workflow orchestration/agent scheduling, real-time status, human approvals and grounded compliance records against non-AI routing.
5. Kill if evidence says vehicles/cold chain/food-safety rules are binding and information is already adequate; do not falsely claim AI automatically reduces hunger.

3. PRIMARY LEGAL REQUIREMENTS AND WHY THEY MATTER
[FSSAI-01, India official Gazette 30 Jul 2019, Schedule I–II] The regulation defines surplus as safe unused food not served to customers, requires advance notice to enable distribution within shelf life, sanitary packing and suitable storage/transport, prepared-food source/date/last consumption date and veg/non-veg labeling, and donor/distributor recordkeeping. Do not promise that reheating makes previously unsafe food safe; an NGO's reported practice is not a validated universal food-safety protocol. Food-safety certification remains human/operator controlled. Confirm any amendments before implementation. https://www.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Gazette_Notification_Surplus_Food_06_08_2019.pdf

5. DIFFERENTIATION AND GENAI — HYPOTHESES, NOT FEATURES TO BUILD

GenAI could parse multilingual voice calls/WhatsApp and unstructured catering records into human-confirmed fields, retrieve relevant authoritative policy and route through approved tools; a stateful agent could request missing facts and generate an auditable case history. If no substantial advantage in safely delivered kg per eligible offer or dispatcher minutes, GenAI innovation FAILS.


6. ENVIRONMENTAL AND SOCIAL OUTCOME DEFINITIONS
Denominator: all donor offers received during defined period; subset independently assessed eligible at offer time; track ineligible separately. Funnel: offer→eligibility decision→recipient acceptance→driver assigned→pickup before deadline→temperature/custody compliance→recipient confirmation→actual served/consumed. Record kg eligible, kg delivered, kg accepted, kg actually served, kg rejected/returned, cost/vehicle-km, and reason codes. Food rescued is NOT automatically incremental emissions avoided: determine what would otherwise happen (discounted sale, self-consumption, other NGO, landfill), displacement of other meals, transport/packaging emissions, and suitable commodity-specific factors. Nutrition and access benefits require verified recipient acceptance and dietary fit. Never equate meals served by Feeding India with recovered food.

Source SG-04: Institute of Policy Studies + Social Service Research Centre, National University of Singapore, Hunger Report III: Landscape Study of Local Food Support (commissioned by The Food Bank Singapore; researchers independent of commissioning charity). Survey 52 food-support providers (7 organizations plus 45 programs) April–November 2025; 26 follow-up provider interviews; desktop research initially identified 173 services across 147 entities, adjusted to 146 entities reportedly offering food support, not all verified active. Original 94-page report https://lkyspp.nus.edu.sg/docs/default-source/ips/hunger-report-part-iii_landscape-study-of-local-food-support.pdf ; project and publication date https://fass.nus.edu.sg/ssr/research-projects/hunger-report-3-project/ . Cite report pages 5–6, 11–13, 28–33, 72–73, 88–89.


Quantified provider-level findings (n=52 survey, SELF-REPORTED, not measured kilograms):
• 42.3% (22/52) said food supply exceeded demand at least occasionally (20 occasionally; 2 frequently), leading to surplus/wastage. This is a proportion of FOOD SUPPORT PROVIDERS, NOT the proportion of food donated, eligible edible kg wasted, or failed donation offers.
• 48.1% (25/52) experienced overlapping services with other providers. Of those 25, 64% (16/25) identified similar food types and 52% (13/25) similar distribution timing. This establishes a coordination signal, NOT that all overlapping support was unnecessary or wasted.
• 82.7% (43/52) reported operational challenges. Among 43, transportation issues 28/43 (65.1%), storage capacity 26/43 (60.5%) and costs 25/43 (58.1%) were the most cited. Among 33 reporting effects on operations, storage was the single most severe challenge for 9/33 (27.3%). Note different denominators; these figures do not imply the same subset of organizations or show why a particular offer failed.
• 61.5% (32/52) said they could consistently meet their stated demand, BUT interviews found some providers cap recipient onboarding to fit resources. This is not proof that underlying community needs were met.


Qualitative causal clues from provider interviews (NOT frequency estimates): missing real-time visibility of donors' excess stock/quantities/timelines caused missed rescue opportunities; providers lacked district-level visibility into overlapping coverage; duplicated cooked meal deliveries sometimes created waste; recipient suitability, changing needs and inability to use donated items complicated outcomes. Providers described unpredictable short-notice collections and needing to decline supply beyond storage capacity. This is unusually direct independent corroboration that information gaps exist IN ADDITION TO physical constraints. However no India or Hyderabad offer-level evidence and no measured effect of coordination software. Authors explicitly caution that provider self-report and sample diversity limit generalizability.
