THE BIG PICTURE
Food can be lost from seed selection and harvesting through transport, retail, quick commerce, kitchens and final disposal. The opportunity is not “another recipe app”: it is to find high-consequence decisions where preventable losses persist despite today's tools, and where reducing friction or fragmented information can measurably change real food outcomes.


WHY THIS MATTERS NOW
UNEP estimates 1.05 billion tonnes of consumer-level food waste in 2022 (households 631m, food service 290m, retail 131m); this includes inedible portions. FAO estimates 13.3% post-harvest/pre-retail food loss globally in 2023. Food loss and waste contribute an estimated 8–10% of global GHG emissions, but these sector totals are NOT AI-addressable savings. India's quick commerce makes purchasing more frequent and immediate: Redseer reported about six quick-commerce orders per monthly transacting user in FY2024, with metros contributing around 90% of that market. Whether this increases food waste is UNPROVEN.


WHO AND WHERE
Farmers → farm workers → aggregators → cold chain → processors → distributors → kiranas/supermarkets/dark stores → delivery workers → household members/cooks → schools/offices/canteens/restaurants/hospitals → food recovery organizations → waste operators. Stakeholders who pay, decide, eat, donate and discard are often different.


INITIAL PROBLEM OPPORTUNITIES (NOT SOLUTIONS)
F01: Multi-person homes: coordination remains plausible, but a 2026 Japan n=126 NON-AI gamified use-first trial physically measured food waste reductions. Any Gemini workflow must beat that baseline.
F02: India 2026 n=363 survey linked impulse online grocery buying with over-ordering; China 2025 study linked online produce buying with vegetable waste. Neither demonstrates India quick-commerce causes discarded food.
F03: One Australian hospital recorded 117.5 kg/day unserved food, preventable fraction UNKNOWN. Beijing workplace study n=656 weighed 78.8g lunch plate waste/diner. Another study of 13,150 corporate meals found 31% estimated plate-waste reduction with NON-AI signage.
F04: Retail/dark stores: short-shelf-life inventory may not clear before spoilage; operational data not public.
F05: Safe surplus: Hyderabad already has NGO vans and discount-resale apps; eligible-offer failures from missing information remain unmeasured.
F06: Upstream: Gujarat tomato cluster 21.51% loss; Northeast India banana n=480 households reported 8.55% post-COVID losses; onion storage trials show major differences by storage method. These noncomparable samples do NOT establish an AI-addressable fraction.
F07: Post-disposal: preventable food is mixed with inedible scraps; recovery value and accountability are difficult to observe.


MEASURABLE ENVIRONMENTAL + SOCIAL OUTCOMES
Baseline and post-change kilograms of AVOIDABLE EDIBLE food wasted; edible surplus safely delivered (not merely listed); cost of discarded food; food servings actually consumed; participation and time burden; verified transport/packaging impact; local waste pathways. Estimate water/CO2e impacts only with suitable food-, region- and counterfactual-specific factors. Food safety, privacy and nutrition are hard constraints.


COMPETITION THAT ALREADY EXISTS
Samsung Food/NoWaste: household planning; non-AI gamified behavior interventions: physically measured household waste reductions. Winnow/Leanpath/Orbisk/SmartQ: kitchen waste tracking and planning; static signs: measured corporate plate-waste effects. MedMeals/Dietech: hospital status sync; Godaam/ICAR/Superplum: storage and cold chain; IFSA/NGOs/Replate: recovery. Generic recipes, inventory, signs, routing, forecasts, storage alerts and donation matching are not novel.


WHERE GENAI COULD MATTER (TO TEST)
Interpreting changing household/kitchen intentions expressed through text, speech, receipts, labels and messy records; reconciling conflicting context; explaining verified trade-offs in local languages. Deterministic code must calculate quantities, enforce food-safety constraints and user permissions, and handle purchase authorizations. GenAI alone cannot know what remains in a refrigerator or certify donated food as safe.


CRITICAL GATE / WHY WE MIGHT REJECT
Prove a consequential residual failure AFTER lists, inventory apps, kitchen forecasting and recovery NGOs. If physical logistics, food safety, user diligence or simple rules dominate, reject GenAI differentiation. Never claim lower hunger or emissions from unexecuted recommendations.


NEXT THREE TESTS
(1) Households: compare physically weighed avoidable waste and user effort against use-first fridge visibility, non-AI gamified reminders, NoWaste/Samsung Food and ordinary Gemini (NOT RUN).
(2) Institutional kitchens: collect 14–28 days of separate edible plate, unserved and preparation waste; benchmark signage, smaller portions, headcounts and deployed vendors BEFORE Gemini (NOT RUN).
(3) Recovery/upstream: audit 20–50 consecutive Hyderabad donor offers with confirmed active volunteer capacity; inspect 20–30 banana/onion/tomato consignments only with authorized event-level data (NOT RUN).


JUDGING FIT (NOT SCORED)
40% GenAI technical: real multimodal parsing + evidence + controls + evaluation. 25% impact: independently measured waste and social benefit. 25% innovation: novel residual workflow vs incumbents. 10% UX: minimal effort. One-week prototype feasibility is a light constraint; no scores until unmet-need evidence exists.
