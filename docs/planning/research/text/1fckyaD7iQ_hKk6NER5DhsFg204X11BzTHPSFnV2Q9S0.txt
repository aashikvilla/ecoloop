1. EXECUTIVE RESEARCH POSITION
The food system is a strong social/environmental domain, but its best AI-addressable gap is NOT YET PROVEN. Priority tiers reflect likely value of the NEXT RESEARCH STEP, not viability or a hackathon winner. Metro-first Indian observation is practical because quick commerce is concentrated there; extension to other JAPAC markets requires fresh evidence.


2. WHAT WE ALREADY KNOW
E01 UNEP Food Waste Index 2024: 1.05bn tonnes global retail/foodservice/household food waste in 2022: 631m household, 290m food services, 131m retail. Includes edible and inedible portions. High-quality environmental scale, not AI-addressable denominator.
E02 FAO 2023 pre-retail food loss ~13.3% globally. Different stage and methodology from UNEP; do not add to UNEP 1.05bn tonnes.
E03 Delhi physical audit, 56 urban households/7 days, 0.749 kg avoidable food waste per household/week; interviews link poor planning, overcooking, lack of coordination and changed work/social plans. Strong specific observation, NOT representative national estimate.
E04 Redseer FY2024 India quick commerce: ~6 orders/month per transacting user, metro share ~90%; says NOTHING about food disposed and not a unique household count.
E05 FSSAI Indian Food Sharing Alliance and 2019 regulations provide established recovery institutions and food-safety requirements; so no claim the recovery market is absent.
E06 NoWaste and Samsung Food already support inventory, collaboration, plans, reminders, AI recommendations; Winnow and Leanpath already measure professional kitchen waste with AI.
E07 EPA 2023 Wasted Food Scale gives high priority to prevention/donation/upcycling over disposal; safety and local context affect choices.
E08 Swiggy 2025 disclosure documents established group ordering, not a missing group-cart feature.


3. OPPORTUNITY HYPOTHESES — PROVISIONAL VERDICTS
FOOD-01 | Household schedule and inventory coordination breakdown | Target: multi-person urban families. Evidence: E03 + Japanese intervention research. Value: weekly avoidable edible kg and grocery cost. Residual gap: can current apps/WhatsApp already handle plan changes? Status ADVANCE TO COMPARATIVE TEST. Confidence: underlying problem medium-high; GenAI edge low. Strongest reject: unchanged behavior/too much data entry. Next test: 10–20 realistic episodes comparing current workflows.
FOOD-02 | Quick-commerce multiple buyers and top-ups vs household consumption | Target: metro users of grocery platforms. Evidence E04 only for purchase frequency; no causal link to edible waste. Status NARROW / UNVERIFIED. Reject if top-up access actually lowers spoilage or existing purchase lists suffice. Test: consenting real household order history + physical waste log, compare non-QC shopping.
FOOD-03 | Meal counts do not match prepared portions in school/college/office/care kitchens | Target: small institutional kitchens. Existing alternatives: headcounts, POS, Winnow, Leanpath. Status ADVANCE TO DATA-ACCESS AUDIT. Reject if simplest attendance forecast works or access impossible. Test: anonymous meal counts + weighed pre/post-service leftovers; separate preparation from plate waste.
FOOD-04 | Retail and dark-store perishables markdown/disposal | Target: grocery operators. Existing inventory and optimization mature, data proprietary. Status NARROW / PARTNER DEPENDENT. Reject if no permissioned shelf-life, stock and shrinkage records. Test: one operator's dated inventory/markdown/waste samples.
FOOD-05 | Safe surplus fails between donor and pickup/recipient | Target: small food operators, verified NGOs, food banks, recipient institutions. Status ADVANCE TO PROCESS AUDIT, not product. Reject if lack of vehicles/cold chain/food-safety eligibility is dominant. Test: 20 actual or documented offers, acceptance, pickup, safe-delivery records; protect recipient privacy.
FOOD-06 | Farm-to-market perishable losses | Target: farmers, aggregators. High burden, equipment/logistics driven. Status DEFER pending one crop + chain. Reject generic farm advisory as saturated/non-actionable.
FOOD-07 | Food expiry/storage misunderstandings | Target: shoppers. Independent Australian label studies exist, but food safety critical and authoritative guidance competes. Status NARROW, compare explanation vs existing guidance, not model safety prediction.
FOOD-08 | After-waste segregation and compost routing | Target: facilities/households. Lower priority than edible-waste prevention unless waste already unavoidable. Status PARK FOR LATER. Reject GenAI novelty if simple signage/logistics solves it.


4. DO-NOT-REVISIT / PRIOR REJECTIONS UNTIL NEW EVIDENCE
R01 Generic AI recipe maker: Samsung Food, NoWaste and general LLMs already do this. Reopen only with independently observed uniquely unserved behavior and outcome.
R02 Universal food inventory app: direct market saturation; photo/receipt scanning and shared lists exist.
R03 “Swiggy/Blinkit integration will auto-buy”: no verified consumer checkout API or partnership; treat as speculative until rights and user-controlled payment confirmed.
R04 Nationwide surplus-food donation map: IFSA, Robin Hood Army, Feeding India and others already operate; only a measured last-mile failure justifies reopening.
R05 Hospital/orphanage/elder-care donation by default: food safety, nutrition, recipient consent and safeguarding make indiscriminate matching unacceptable.
R06 Assume every leftover can be donated: inaccurate; FSSAI defines surplus as safe, unused and not served; recording/storage/temperature matter.
R07 Multiply total global waste by arbitrary percent and call it TAM, revenue, or proven emissions savings: prohibited.
R08 Assume q-commerce induces waste because orders are frequent: causal direction unknown; could reduce bulk overbuying.
R09 Score “one-week feasibility” as major veto: framework says light sanity check only; distinguish valuable problem from prototype slice.


5. PROBLEM VALUE SCORE AND JUDGING RUBRIC
Score ONLY when supported on urgency/consequences (15), frequency/intensity (10), affected population (10), workaround cost/willingness spend (10), independent evidence (15), unmet need (15), why now (5), access to user/data/artefacts (5), measurable outcome (5), track alignment (10). CURRENT STATE: scores withheld for all food hypotheses due to unresolved unmet need and workflow-specific frequency.
Judge rubric (SEPARATE): Technical + GenAI 40%; problem alignment/impact 25%; innovation 25%; UX 10%. Cannot meaningfully score a solution not yet built.
Hard rejects: unsupported impact claim, no demonstrable residual gap, unsafe donation/food claims, inadequate evidence for AI-specific task, inability to demonstrate core workflow, event rule violation.

FOOD-03B — HOSPITAL MEAL-STATUS EXCEPTIONS: Ni et al. (Waste Management issue 15 Jan 2026; ePub 12 Nov 2025) weighed 134.1 kg/day kitchen food waste at one Australian hospital food service, of which 117.5 kg/day was unserved, equal to 149.3 g per meal PRODUCED. 2,214 returned plates audited; ward plate waste averaged 0.7 kg per patient-DAY. One site also served eight external sites, and unserved food is not automatically avoidable. No hospital dataset access or model benchmark exists. Verdict NARROW / CONDITIONAL DATA ACCESS AUDIT, not product. FOOD-01 — NON-GENAI INTERVENTION SETS A STRONGER BASELINE. Seta et al. (Detritus 35, published May 26 2026) assigned 126 Japanese households to intervention/control (63 each) and physically monitored avoidable waste with connected weighing over baseline, intervention and three-month follow-up measurement periods. Average weekly household avoidable waste decreased 190g (45%) in intervention vs 60g (13%) in control; baseline-adjusted bootstrap GLM reports a significant between-group difference. The 32 percentage points compare WITHIN-GROUP PERCENT CHANGES, not an AI benefit or a directly estimated treatment effect in kg. No demonstrated persistence beyond the reported short measurement windows or India generalization. A low-tech/gamified use-first and visibility approach is now a mandatory baseline. Strongest kill: if visibility, reminders and habit changes already prevent the avoidable grams at lower burden, GenAI lacks novelty. Source E046 https://digital.detritusjournal.com/articles/effects-of-a-gamified-self-monitoring-app-on-household-food-waste-reduction/5732 . Verdict FOOD-01 ADVANCE ONLY TO NON-AI COMPARATOR VALIDATION; T14 NOT RUN.


FOOD-02 — INDIA-SPECIFIC OVER-ORDERING MECHANISM, NOT WASTE CAUSALITY. Jasrotia et al. (SN Business & Economics 6(10), Oct 2026) surveyed 363 Indian online grocery shoppers using PLS-SEM; perceived platform value and impulse shopping were associated with over-ordering. The study did NOT weigh discarded food, establish causality or isolate rapid quick commerce. LocalCircles 2023 self-selected produce survey indicates many respondents' primary produce source was offline and online quality/returns complaints are common; this cannot be extrapolated to 2026 market share or edible spoilage. Strongest kill: excess orders are consumed, preserved or replaced, and no incremental edible waste remains. Verdict FOOD-02 NARROW, pair actual order motives, freshness and weighed disposal; T10 NOT RUN. Sources E047 https://doi.org/10.1007/s43546-026-01458-1 ; E055 https://www.localcircles.com/a/press/page/grocery-platforms-survey .


FOOD-03C — WORKPLACE PLATE WASTE IS MEASURED, BUT SIMPLE SIGNS ALREADY WORK. Zhang et al. (Frontiers, Dec 3 2024) directly weighed 656 samples at FOUR Beijing buffet-style workplace canteens (2021 observations); mean lunch plate waste 78.8g/diner, mean daily across meals 158.4g/diner. Two weekdays/site, not Indian kitchen overproduction. Food-specific footprints in that paper are MODELLED embedded burdens, not reductions. Independent counterfactual evidence: Daker et al. (Scientific Reports Dec 24 2025) analyzed 13,150 anonymous corporate dining scans across sites in five European/US countries and estimated 31% less plate waste vs no-signage counterfactual with static social-norm and portion-choice prompts. This was NON-AI. Existing Orbisk/SmartQ/Winnow/Leanpath already offer plate or kitchen management; Compass claims require independent verification. The two studies are not pooled because populations, interventions and denominators differ. Strongest kill: signs, half-portions and routine headcounts address most avoidable waste. Verdict FOOD-03C NARROW / NON-AI RED TEAM, T11 NOT RUN. Sources E048 https://doi.org/10.3389/fsufs.2024.1455756 ; E049 https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-28175-3 ; E050 https://www.thesmartq.com/ ; E051 https://compass-group.co.in/sustainability/ .


FOOD-05B — REAL CAPACITY, NOT REGISTERED VOLUNTEERS. Tanksale et al. (Int J Production Economics 277:109385, 2024) modeled an Indian foodbank network using mixed real and estimated inputs and reported one representative's anecdote of roughly 200 registered volunteers sometimes shrinking to single-digit active capacity. This is ONE anecdote, not Hyderabad prevalence. The paper already models capacity, nutrition and equitable access with operations research; an AI volunteer-matcher is not inherently novel. Strongest kill: trucks, storage, time windows and recipient availability are binding, or a live roster plus deterministic optimization suffices. Verdict FOOD-05B NARROW, T12 NOT RUN. Source E052 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpe.2024.109385 .


FOOD-06C — BANANA FIRST-MILE LOSSES, NOT AN AI-SAVING PERCENTAGE. Das (World Food Policy, May 13 2025) surveyed 480 banana-farming households in Meghalaya/Assam, reporting 23.74% postharvest losses during COVID disruption vs 8.55% post-COVID. Strongest kill: roads, credit, storage and already-deployed cold chain dominate, leaving no feasible missed information decision. Verdict FOOD-06C NARROW / DATA ACCESS, T13 NOT RUN. Sources E053 https://doi.org/10.1002/wfp2.70006 ; E054 https://superplum.com/ .
