EXECUTIVE AUDIT & RESEARCH RULES
The prior dashboard recorded 22 queued questions, 36 source entries and five competitors, but lacked full dossiers, explicit decisions and trustworthy status columns. Source count is not equivalent to independent evidence. Several rows of the Research Queue were shifted into wrong columns, and Q-011 was overwritten. Those are data-quality issues, not just research shortages.
This report repairs the unit of research: one specific person/organization, one job, one failure mechanism, one measurable outcome, existing alternatives, contrary evidence, and a next falsification test. The Google Cloud capability review is separate from the problem-value case.
Definitions: Problem exposure evidence means a problem exists for some people; workflow evidence identifies precisely where; alternative-gap evidence shows a defensible unmet need. Only the last type justifies shortlist advancement. Source types and dates are included. No interviews, experiments, or customer validation have been conducted by us in this pass.
Status meanings: ADVANCE TO VALIDATION = worth targeted next-pass research, NOT validated unmet need. NARROW = broad hypothesis is too vague. DEFER = insufficient causal/market evidence for this use case. REJECT GENERIC FORM = broad solution framing is already well served. Scores /100 remain unassigned.
SIZING RULE: national complaint totals / global disability numbers / company counts are not automatically TAM; denominator is only the population or cases in the specific workflow. Until actual incidence and paying/adopting population are known, sizing is stated as a formula with unknown variables.


TRACK 1 — BFSI


BFSI-01 | SCAM-INDUCED AUTHORIZED PAYMENT TRANSFERS: INTERVENTION AND RECOVERY
Who and job: Bank consumers who are induced to send money; fraud/anti-scam operations staff attempting to interrupt transfers and coordinate response with authorities.
Quantified burden (official primary reporting): Singapore Police's Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025 recorded 37,308 scam cases, down 27.6% year over year, and SGD 913.1 million lost, down 17.9% from 2024. The police said 81.8% of scam cases involved self-effected transfers. Australia’s National Anti-Scam Centre combined five reporting channels and recorded 481,523 reports and AUD 2.18 billion in reported losses in 2025. These datasets are different jurisdictions and definitions; do not sum or extrapolate.
Specific research hypothesis: The consequential step may be the period between receiving a suspicious request and authorizing payment, plus evidence handoff after funds leave, rather than failure to classify a suspicious message. This is a HYPOTHESIS, not a measured failure rate.
Why it might persist: Social engineering can persuade customers to approve transfers; transaction controls and message classification cannot always observe intent. Verify the causal chain in case-level sources.
What already exists / counterevidence: Government ScamShield checks calls, messages, screenshots and URLs using AI; has 24/7 hotline and bank links. Singapore Police report at least SGD 348m in potential losses avoided via proactive interventions in 2025, and scam case volumes fell sharply. General scam detection is therefore not a novel unmet need.
Sizing/impact: number of intervention-eligible fraudulent authorized transfers × prevented loss per correct intervention; case counts and gross reported loss are upper-context only. No conversion assumption validated.
Biggest rejection reason: Existing banks, police and ScamShield already provide strong interventions; data access, privacy and proving incremental benefit may defeat a newcomer.
Confidence: HIGH scam burden, MEDIUM role of persuasion, LOW residual product gap. Decision: NARROW.
Next falsification: Singapore Police brief sections on self-effected transfers, complaint pathways and proactive alerts; independently assess where false negatives/missed escalations persist. Compare with existing ScamShield Q1 2025 performance and support limitations.
Sources: [B1] Singapore Police, Annual Scam and Cybercrime Brief 2025 (released Feb 2026) https://www.police.gov.sg/Media-Hub/Police-Life/2026/02/Scams-and-Cybercrime-Fell-by-Almost-a-Quarter-in-2025 ; [B2] ACCC, Targeting Scams 2025 (30 Mar 2026) https://www.accc.gov.au/media-release/continued-action-critical-to-combat-fraud-as-annual-scam-losses-exceed-2-billion ; [B3] GovTech Singapore, ScamShield (updated 28 Jul 2026) https://www.tech.gov.sg/products-and-services/for-citizens/scam-prevention/scamshield/ ; [B4] Singapore OGP product report, Q1 2025 https://reports.open.gov.sg/scamshield/overview


BFSI-04 | BANKING COMPLAINTS INVOLVING LOANS AND DIGITAL SERVICES IN INDIA
Who and job: Customers pursuing a resolution with banks and the RBI Ombudsman; bank grievance officers assembling evidence and meeting deadlines.
Quantified burden: RBI annual report FY2024-25 states 2.96 lakh complaints were received by RBI Ombudsman offices, compared with 2.93 lakh the prior year, mainly concerning loans/advances and digital banking products. This is not the total number of underlying banking problems in India.
Potential failure mechanism: The complaint might require reconciling communications, terms, evidence and regulatory requirements; this remains an INFERENCE, not demonstrated from complaint-level records.
Existing alternatives: RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, bank complaint systems, consumer helplines, financial advisers and lawyers. Complaint volume alone is not proof these remedies fail.
Sizing: annual eligible unresolved cases in a chosen grievance category × per-case dispute cost/turnaround burden; category denominator, closure rate, and cost remain unknown.
Counterevidence: A functioning regulator pathway already exists and a small increase in complaints does not establish deteriorating service.
Biggest rejection reason: Most delays could be governed by policy, bank incentives or authority, rather than AI or document interpretation.
Confidence: HIGH complaint count, LOW root-cause specificity. Decision: NARROW.
Next falsification: retrieve category-level RBI Ombudsman report, complaint disposal/appeal distributions, process documents and consumer case reports; compare commercial grievance portals.
Source: [B5] RBI Annual Report 2024–25, section VI.98, https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/AnnualReportPublications.aspx?Id=1436


TRACK 2 — RETAIL & COMMERCE


RET-01 | BUYERS FAIL TO FIND COMPATIBLE SPARE PARTS AND ACCESSORIES
Who and job: Shoppers who know a device/model/problem but not the replacement-part name; retailers maintaining product catalogs and compatible SKU relations.
Quantified mechanism: Baymard Institute's 2026 search benchmark (170+ sites/apps, 10,000+ UX ratings; largely Western benchmark) finds 56% inadequately support search overall, and 44% have issues with compatibility searches. Specific Dell adapter examples show irrelevant/incompatible recommendations. These are sample-site percentages, NOT a measure of failed orders, returns or JAPAC prevalence.
Why it might persist: device-part relations are multi-attribute and model-specific; sparse catalog metadata or misleading display attributes can defeat semantic matching. This is plausible and supported qualitatively in the Baymard article, but prevalence in JAPAC remains unmeasured.
Alternatives and counterevidence: Google AI Commerce Search and Algolia AI Search already offer semantic intent, hybrid/vector matching and catalog search. OEM parts finders and manual customer support serve compatibility needs. Baymard documents some retailers with effective compatibility display. A generic AI shopping assistant therefore lacks differentiation.
Sizing/impact: targeted site sessions involving compatibility × failed retrieval rate × abandonment or return cost; all variables need local testing.
Biggest rejection reason: Mature search vendors and OEM fitment databases could outperform an LLM on hard compatibility requirements.
Confidence: HIGH controlled-site UX issues, LOW JAPAC transfer, LOW residual gap. Decision: ADVANCE TO LOCAL VALIDATION; REJECT GENERIC CHATBOT.
Next falsification: Build a neutral benchmark of 30–50 actual accessory queries on leading India/SEA/Japan/ANZ catalogs; manually verify ground-truth compatibility and compare search and OEM finders. Do not invent user reviews.
Sources: [R1] Baymard, updated 29 Apr 2026 https://baymard.com/research-articles/ecommerce-search-query-types ; [R2] Google Cloud AI Commerce Search https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-cx/ai-commerce-search ; [R3] Algolia AI Search https://www.algolia.com/products/ai-search


RET-02 | MICRO-MERCHANT DIGITAL TOOL ADOPTION BARRIERS, CAMBODIA
Who and job: Cambodian microenterprises, including micro-retailers, attempting to use digital tools in day-to-day sales and operations.
Quantified study: ADB's Dec 2025 country study of over 400 MSMEs reports micro businesses are less likely to adopt operational technology and identifies skills shortages, cybersecurity concerns and infrastructure constraints. The source is not retail-only and does not quantify lost sales due to one specific workflow.
Cross-country context: ADB's 2025 Asia SME Monitor covers 26 economies and estimates MSMEs are 99.8% of firms on average in its coverage. That is not a software customer count or market forecast.
Alternatives/counterevidence: training, financing, off-the-shelf POS systems, merchant marketplaces, agencies and policy programs. ADB itself recommends training and support; some constraints may not be technical.
Sizing: eligible micro-retailers in one country × actual proportion experiencing one defined workflow failure × realistic annual loss/price; presently unsized.
Biggest rejection reason: Root cause may be skills, affordability or infrastructure, making another platform inappropriate.
Confidence: MEDIUM adoption barriers, LOW precise workflow gap. Decision: NARROW.
Next falsification: segment the >400 SME survey to retail, firm size and use-case; identify vendors/solutions active in Cambodian MSMEs; locate observed merchant interviews or third-party product reviews.
Sources: [R4] ADB, 2025-12 https://www.adb.org/publications/digital-adoption-cambodian-msmes ; [R5] ADB SME Monitor, 2025-11 https://www.adb.org/publications/asia-sme-monitor-2025


TRACK 3 — MANUFACTURING


MFG-01 | SMALL-FACTORY MAINTENANCE FAILURES AND HISTORICAL KNOWLEDGE
Who and job: Maintenance technicians, shift leads and supervisors in smaller Indian factories diagnosing failures and documenting repairs.
Quantified evidence: WEF/IndiaAI 2025 SME AI playbook presents an Indian gearbox maker case reporting roughly 40% fewer unplanned breakdowns and 30% lower maintenance costs after applying sensors and ML. This is a SINGLE SUCCESS STORY, not evidence those effect sizes are typical or that failures are unsolved. WEF also emphasizes cost, skill, awareness and organizational constraints.
Mechanism to test: fault histories, sensor records and manuals may be hard to reconcile in small factories, leading to repeated troubleshooting. Specific frequency, downtime hours and rework remain UNKNOWN.
Alternatives/counterevidence: MaintainX work orders, Fiix/CMMS, OEM maintenance guides, predictive sensors and spreadsheets exist; the published success case shows existing technology can work.
Sizing: facilities in one selected sector × annual preventable hours × fully burdened hourly downtime cost; all need sector-verified values.
Biggest rejection reason: sensor/CMMS adoption and data readiness—not intelligence—is the binding constraint; competitors cover routine work.
Confidence: MEDIUM for technological possibility/constraints, LOW for remaining unmet workflow. Decision: NARROW.
Next falsification: independently source failure logs from one machine class; compare actual CMMS capabilities and non-digital root causes; assess whether technicians have usable historical data.
Sources: [M1] WEF/IndiaAI, AI Playbook for India SMEs, PDF foreword May 2025, site posted Oct 2025 https://reports.weforum.org/docs/WEF_Transforming_Small_Businesses_2025.pdf ; [M2] MaintainX work order documentation https://help.getmaintainx.com/about-work-orders ; [M3] ADB, MSME digitalization and skills context, Aug 2025 https://www.adb.org/publications/strengthening-the-ecosystem-of-vibrant-msm-es-for-resilient-growth-in-asia-and-the-pacific


MFG-03 | WORKER-SIDE TECHNOLOGY ADOPTION IN SMALL INDUSTRIAL OPERATIONS
Who and job: Factory line staff and supervisors transferring observations into standardized maintenance and quality records, sometimes in multilingual environments.
Evidence: WEF 2024 frontline-worker study identifies worker adoption and change-management as material issues; ADB Cambodia >400 MSME study identifies lack of skills, security concerns and infrastructure as digital uptake constraints. Neither establishes a manufacturing-specific percentage of unrecorded observations. Combining them suggests, but does not verify, a last-mile adoption mechanism.
Alternatives: mobile CMMS, structured checklists, voice dictation, supervisor rounds and workforce training.
Consequence/scale: affected workers × omitted or delayed critical records × cost of repeat intervention. No reliable inputs yet.
Counterevidence: worker training and redesigning forms may be cheaper than complex AI; evidence is cross-sector and qualitative.
Biggest rejection reason: No direct operational measure; risk of inventing a problem from generalized change-management language.
Confidence: LOW-MEDIUM. Decision: DEFER pending plant-level corroboration.
Next falsification: obtain one published plant case with event-reporting loss, line-level studies and real employee workflow. Compare mobile alternatives.
Sources: [M4] WEF, manufacturing frontline (8 Jan 2024) https://www.weforum.org/publications/views-from-the-manufacturing-front-line-workers-insights-on-how-to-introduce-new-technology ; [R4] ADB Cambodia 2025.


TRACK 4 — MEDIA, CONTENT & DIGITAL EXPERIENCES


MED-03 | DIGITAL CONTENT FAILS BASIC ACCESSIBILITY REQUIREMENTS
Who and job: People using screen readers/keyboard navigation and content publishers responsible for accessible pages; includes educational content, commerce and news, not limited to media.
Quantified evidence: WebAIM automated audit of one million high-ranking home pages in Feb 2026 found 95.9% had detected WCAG 2 failures, 56.1 detectable errors per home page on average. Missing form labels affected 51% of sampled home pages; missing alternative text 53.1%. This is a global, automated HOME PAGE audit and does not measure the proportion of actual users blocked, deeper workflows or all violations.
Why it persists: content complexity, design/development practices, fragmented ownership and downstream publishing processes are possibilities; WebAIM itself warns that automated detection covers only a subset of accessibility issues and correlations are not causal.
Alternatives/counterevidence: WAVE, axe accessibility testing, browser tooling, WCAG and manual accessibility auditors are established. A generic auto-alt-text generator is not an established unmet gap.
Sizing/impact: audited media/learning properties with material user task failures × remediation effort and denied task outcomes, NOT one million home pages.
Biggest rejection reason: Automated testing already identifies common errors; publishing ownership and remediation incentives could dominate.
Confidence: HIGH accessibility failure detection, LOW new market opportunity. Decision: ADVANCE TO WORKFLOW INVESTIGATION, not product selection.
Next falsification: review accessibility issue trackers for major APAC content services; distinguish unsolved multimedia access and complex stateful forms from automatically detectable HTML basics. Test real tasks with assistive tech or find independent task studies.
Source: [D1] WebAIM Million 2026 (Feb measurements, 2026) https://webaim.org/projects/million/?target=_blank


MED-02 | LANGUAGE-OF-INSTRUCTION BARRIERS AND LEARNING CONTENT
Who and job: Learners in non-dominant language communities, teachers preparing appropriate materials, local publishers and education administrators.
Quantified evidence: UNESCO's APAC multilingual education group states 30% of South Asian learners and 37% of East/Southeast Asia/Pacific learners are taught in a language they do not understand. These are broad regional indicators, not direct counts of missing app translations, and not a number to apply to a given product's TAM.
Root causes: language policy, teacher capacity, curriculum, publishing economics and community language diversity. Exact weights differ by country and need documentation.
Alternatives and counterevidence: mother-tongue education policy, teacher training, community publishers, machine translation, UNESCO LearnBig initiative with reported 2,000+ books and 526,000 reached learners (program claim, not independently audited learning impact).
Sizing/impact: learners in a particular language-grade-curriculum pair × empirically missing usable material × measurable learning outcome. Language group and intervention absent.
Biggest rejection reason: School-system policy and teacher availability may dominate; generic translation unlikely to solve learning outcomes.
Confidence: HIGH regional exposure indicator, LOW platform gap. Decision: NARROW TO A COUNTRY/LANGUAGE/CONTENT TYPE.
Next falsification: compare publicly listed materials for two minority-language curricula, local teaching practice studies, translation quality and digital platform coverage.
Sources: [D2] UNESCO APAC Multilingual Education Working Group https://www.unesco.org/sdg4education2030/en/regional-coordination/asia-pacific/about/regoional-and-sub-regional-coordination/mlewg ; [D3] UNESCO LearnBig (19 Feb 2026) https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/expanding-access-learning-through-digital-innovation-unescos-learnbig-initiative


TRACK 5 — FUTURE OF WORK & ENTERPRISE PRODUCTIVITY


WORK-01 | KNOWLEDGE RETRIEVAL INTERRUPTS WORK
Who and job: Cross-functional knowledge workers trying to locate the current, permission-appropriate policy, decision, document or issue context to complete a task.
Quantified evidence: Atlassian's 2025 State of Teams reports surveying 12,000 workers and 200 executives and describes 25% of time wasted searching for answers (vendor survey). Microsoft's Work Trend Index 2025 surveyed 31,000 across 31 markets and reports 48% describe work as chaotic and fragmented. Different vendors, but both have commercial incentives; neither measures APAC-specific failure of a particular system.
Mechanism to test: misaligned records/permissions, stale decisions, vocabulary and handoff gaps; not all are retrieval failures.
Alternatives/counterevidence: Microsoft 365 Copilot Search (100+ connectors), Atlassian Rovo Search/agents and Glean enterprise search/action all offer grounded, permission-aware retrieval; Glean reports 275+ connectors. Generic enterprise RAG is saturated.
Sizing: observed searches for one repeated job × net lost minutes × burdened cost; survey-time percentages are not measured workflow values.
Biggest rejection reason: direct incumbents already cover basic discovery; survey results may describe coordination culture rather than a search feature.
Confidence: MEDIUM perceived workload, LOW uncovered retrieval need. Decision: REJECT GENERIC SEARCH; NARROW TO ONE UNSERVED DECISION WORKFLOW.
Next falsification: compare actual exception handling across procurement, compliance or IT incidents against Copilot/Rovo/Glean documented functions, and obtain task-level evidence of failed retrieval/action.
Sources: [W1] Atlassian State of Teams 2025 https://www.atlassian.com/blog/state-of-teams-2025 ; [W2] Microsoft WTI 2025 https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born ; [W3] Microsoft Copilot Search docs https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/connectors/copilot-search ; [W4] Atlassian Rovo docs https://support.atlassian.com/rovo/docs/explore-rovo-features/ ; [W5] Glean docs https://docs.glean.com/connectors/about

TRACK 6 — SUSTAINABILITY & SOCIAL IMPACT


SUS-01 | WARNING EXISTS BUT EXPOSED HOUSEHOLD CANNOT TAKE PROTECTIVE ACTION
Who and job: Lower-income, flood-exposed residents and local emergency coordinators deciding whether/when/how to evacuate.
Evidence: WMO's 2025 global review reports 72% of APAC countries have multi-hazard warning systems (a country-level indicator). ADB's Asian Development Policy Report 2025 cites Thailand flood displacement analysis: about 10 percentage points more low-wealth households stayed at home during floods versus high-wealth households due to resource limitations. That is a specific subgroup and geographic observation, not a region-wide evacuation rate.
Root-cause hypothesis: transport, financial resources, dependent care, shelter access and trust may block timely protective action even with forecast availability; needs local causal verification.
Alternatives/counterevidence: national hydrometeorological and emergency services, WMO/CREWS last-mile programs and Google Flood Hub forecasting. Google offers over 150-country forecasts, but API access is pilot-restricted; some forecast locations have low verified confidence. Existing services mean 'another flood prediction app' is not a clear gap.
Sizing/impact: exposed low-income households in a named floodplain × observed non-evacuation due to addressable constraints × outcomes prevented; no numbers yet for one location.
Biggest rejection reason: constraints are physical resources and governance, not missing information; no ethically credible measured benefit from a software prototype.
Confidence: HIGH hazard and system observations, MEDIUM Thailand disparity indicator, LOW actionable gap. Decision: ADVANCE TO ROOT-CAUSE RESEARCH, no solution.
Next falsification: find Thai flood evacuation field study and NGO/government retrospective with reasons for remaining; distinguish warning receipt, comprehension, ability, and outcome. Inventory existing evacuation/shelter support.
Sources: [S1] WMO, Global Status Multi-Hazard EWS (12 Nov 2025) https://wmo.int/news/media-centre/early-warning-systems-reach-new-heights-critical-gaps-jeopardize-global-progress ; [S2] ADB Asian Development Policy Report 2025 https://www.adb.org/adpr/editions/digital-transformation ; [S3] Google Flood Hub FAQ https://support.google.com/flood-hub/answer/16364606?hl=en ; [S4] Google Flood API access FAQ https://support.google.com/flood-hub/answer/16364306?hl=en


SUS-03 | WALKING-ROUTE SAFETY/ACCESS INFORMATION (MAPMYWALK UNDER REVIEW)
Who and job: People walking through specific neighborhoods, choosing among routes, and checking in with trusted contacts.
Existing project plan: proposes route comparison using lighting, recent incidents and open establishments, live updates and timed SOS. Critically, the plan itself requires a neighborhood-level source audit and clear labeling of synthetic incident/lighting data. CROSS-CUTTING OPPORTUNITY OBSERVATIONS (NOT SOLUTIONS)
A. Information availability vs ability to act: warning/financial grievance/knowledge search. Finding information is often not the actual bottleneck; permissions, incentives, resources and last-mile steps matter.
B. Existing technology vs adoption: manufacturing CMMS, retail search products and small-business digital platforms are mature. Market gaps need segment-level proof.
C. Accessibility vs mere content generation: repeated WCAG issues and multilingual learning gaps persist, but automated validators and translation systems already exist.
D. Low-risk and high-risk systems differ: incorrect compatibility search wastes time/money; incorrect fraud, flood or walking advice can cause significant harm. Validation and human responsibility are central.
E. Cross-market statistics cannot be pooled without consistent definitions.

SECONDARY EVIDENCE ADDENDUM — JAPAC LOCALIZATION (12 OCTOBER 2026)


MED-03 / INDIA — EARLY LOCAL CORROBORATION, NOT A VALIDATED MARKET GAP
BarrierBreak and NCPEDP published the second BB100 Indian digital accessibility audit in November 2025 (updated May 2026). Their automated audit of the 100 most-visited Indian websites reported an average of 116.40 detectable accessibility errors per home page, including 285.20 for entertainment and 121.48 for ecommerce. This study was conducted by an accessibility vendor and uses a different tool, sample and measurement than WebAIM Million; never compare the absolute averages as if they are from identical methods. This provides a separate, India-specific signal that accessibility defects exist in high-traffic content experiences.
The Indian GIGW 3.0 national guidelines explicitly cover government websites and apps and draw on WCAG. A government e-governance portal publicly acknowledges that some PDF documents and Hindi-language information are inaccessible (first-party site statement; retrieve directly where accessible). That is a concrete disclosure of a possible content-format/localization gap, not a population prevalence estimate.
Critically, SugamyaWeb and BarrierBreak A11yInspect/remediation services already offer accessibility scans and corrective guidance in India. The question to validate is now WHY publishers have unremediated task-blocking barriers despite accessibility standards, scanning tools and commercial support. Measuring error detection alone would not demonstrate novelty.
